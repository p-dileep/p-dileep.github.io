The game

56 is a trick-taking game for two teams. The Jack is the highest card, then the nine. Before play there is an auction, and the bids are a language: the way you name a suit tells your partners what you hold. Learning that language is most of the game.

What it does

  • Tables for six players or eight, in the browser, with nothing to install and no account.
  • The bidding language, including the signals partners send each other.
  • Chips carried from hand to hand, with doubling and redoubling.
  • Computer players with names and temperaments: some cautious, some bold, most in between.

How the computer players think

A computer player listens to the auction the way a person does. From each bid it works out which cards the others are likely to hold. Before making a big bid it deals out the unseen cards many times over, plays each deal through, and bids only if its team wins often enough to be worth the risk. I tuned that threshold by having the computer players play each other over and over.
